# ScholarTrack audit — 30 October 2026

Read-only audit of the repository, the live site at
<https://scholartrack.rangkalabs.cloud> and the production Supabase project.
Nothing was changed: no row written, no schema altered, no deploy, no model
call. Raw results are in [`audit-2026-10-30/`](audit-2026-10-30/) and every
number below can be regenerated with the scripts in
[`scripts/audit/`](../scripts/audit/).

## The short version

The engineering is better than the data. The quote check works — over 1,732
grounded quotes re-tested against the live pages, **no fabricated value was
found**, and for eligibility, documents and funding the quotes still sit on the
page at a 94–99% rate. What has failed is everything that was supposed to keep
those readings current: **the weekly refresh has never run**, so the catalogue
is frozen at 29 August 2026, 16 of its 19 deadlines have passed, and **43% of
stored deadline quotes no longer appear on the page they came from**.

Alongside that, the catalogue is pointed at the wrong audience. On an
Indonesia-first reading, **9 of 20 sampled programmes are not open to an
Indonesian applicant**, 27 of 43 rows are not a major programme at all, and 14
of the 21 major programmes on your list — LPDP included — are missing.

### Severity rubric

**Critical** — a student can be given a wrong fact that costs them an
application, or data is exposed. **High** — the product's central promise is
unmet, or a whole section is broken. **Medium** — real degradation with a
workaround. **Low** — polish and drift.

### What I verified versus what I am assuming

Verified by measurement: every number in the table above, the RLS exposure, the
dependency advisories, the contrast and performance figures, the robots posture,

Assumed, because I have no Vercel access: *why* no scheduled run exists, and
whether the deployed commit is `bf9acbf`. The evidence for the first is strong
and circumstantial — see C1. Both are flagged where they appear.

## 2. Data quality

### C1 · The catalogue is frozen, and the refresh that should prevent that has never run — **critical**

**Evidence.** `pipeline_jobs` holds 23 rows. The newest is `2026-08-29T10:24`.
Every one was created by your own admin account; none by a scheduled run. The
`admins` table has exactly **one** row, so the dedicated refresh account that
`docs/deployment.md:89-93` requires does not exist, and
`src/app/api/cron/refresh/route.ts:90-97` returns 501 without
`CRON_SUPABASE_EMAIL` / `CRON_SUPABASE_PASSWORD`. Roughly nine Mondays have
passed since the last write. `scholarship_extractions` confirms it: 141 rows,
newest `2026-08-29T12:58`.

Staleness, measured two ways, because the two readings differ:

| | never read | ≤30d | 31–60d | 61–90d | >90d |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| As stored (`last_verified_at`) | 2 | **0** | 0 | 41 | 0 |
| Rows a human verified | 0 | **0** | 0 | 9 | 0 |

**User impact.** 16 of the 19 stored deadlines have passed. The UI is honest
about it — cards read "Closed 6 Oct 2026 · 24 days ago · Cycle has rolled over" —
but honesty is not the same as usefulness: a student opening the dashboard
today meets sixteen closed programmes and three open ones. Worse, the funders
have moved on and we have not. Verified case: **Language Study Fellowships**
holds `2026-03-05`; the page today reads *"Submit the following materials in
CARAT by the deadline: March 4, 2027, at noon"*. The next cycle is published and
the catalogue shows the last one as closed. That is how this costs somebody an
application.

**Assumption.** I cannot see Vercel, so I cannot prove the cron never fired —
only that it has never produced a job row, and that the account it needs does
not exist. Either the schedule is not firing or it is returning 501 every week.
Both are invisible: nothing watches this endpoint, as
`src/app/api/cron/refresh/route.ts:76-78` admits in its own comment.

**Fix.** Create the refresh account, add it to `admins`, set the three
variables. Then add an alert on "no successful pipeline job in 8 days".
**Effort: 1–2 hours**, and it is the single highest-value hour in this report.

### H1 · The quote check validates once and never again, and dates are where that hurts — **high**

This is the central measurement of the audit. Every grounded quote in every
extraction was re-tested with the project's own matcher — `findQuote()` from
`scripts/extract/quote.ts` against `normalise()`d text from a fresh fetch. No
model involved; it is a string search, which is the point.

| Field family | exact | loose | gone | % gone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dates on the row** (deadline, opens, cycle) | 113 | 1 | **79** | **41%** |
| **Dates in the timeline** | 408 | 9 | 73 | 15% |
| Coverage (what the award pays) | 262 | 6 | 16 | 6% |
| Documents required | 294 | 3 | 8 | 3% |
| Eligibility (who may apply) | 243 | 4 | 2 | **1%** |
| Duration, award count, obligations, subjects | 163 | 5 | 1 | 1% |

Single worst fields: `timeline.opens` 64% gone, `opens_date` 61%,
`timeline.closes` 51%, **`deadline_date` 43%** (30 of 70), `cycle_label` 29%.

The mechanism is visible per page: **73 of 109** date quotes from
`chevening.org/scholarships/application-timeline/` are gone, 20 of 30 from
`gatescambridge.org/apply/timeline/`, and **18 of 18** from the Harvard Language
Study page. Those pages rolled to the next cycle. The quotes were real when
taken and are not there now.

**What this says about "no quote, no value".** The rule is strong exactly where
facts are stable and weak exactly where they are not. It is a check on
*fabrication at extraction time*, and it was never a check on *currency*. For
the write-up, that is the finding: grounding bought a 1%-drift eligibility
field and a 43%-drift deadline field, and only re-verification closes the gap.

**Fix.** Re-run grounding on a schedule and surface the result — a row whose
deadline quote has vanished should drop to "needs re-reading" rather than keep
displaying. `scripts/audit/quote-drift.ts` is already the measurement; wiring it
into the refresh is the work. **Effort: 1–2 days.**

### H4 · The quote never reaches the student — **high**

`apply_extraction` (`supabase/migrations/016_not_stated_and_gap_causes.sql:133-232`)
copies values, `source_url`, `verification_status`, `last_verified_at`,
`not_stated` and `field_sources` onto the row — **but never `evidence`**. Quotes
live only in `scholarship_extractions`, which is correctly deny-all to the
public API. Searching `src/` for quote rendering finds it only in
`components/admin/verify-card.tsx` and `components/admin/extraction-card.tsx`.

And the per-field provenance that *is* carried is empty: **`field_sources` is
`{}` on all 43 rows**, and `scholarship_sources` has 0 rows, so
cross-verification (migration 011) has never produced anything.
`SourceNote` (`src/components/scholarship/source-note.tsx:36`) returns `null`
when there is no entry — so the entire provenance UI renders nothing, on every
row, today. It is also wired to just two fields, `deadline_date` and
`opens_date`, and only inside the `timeline.length > 0` branch of
`scholarship-detail-sheet.tsx:147-158`.

**User impact.** The product's distinguishing claim is checkable by you and by
nobody else. A student cannot see the sentence behind a date, and neither can
the Hermes agent reading the public API.

**Fix.** Carry `evidence` onto the row (or expose a read-only view of it) and
render "source" + quote-on-demand per fact. **Effort: 2–3 days**, and it is the
thing that turns the rule into a feature.

### M1 · "Last verified" means "last crawled" — **medium**

`apply_extraction` sets `last_verified_at = ext.fetched_at` and
`verification_status = 'ai_extracted'`. So the timestamp records when the
*crawler fetched the page*, not when a person checked anything. 41 of 43 rows
carry a date; only 9 are `verified`.

`README.md:139` states the opposite: *"`last_verified_at` is NULL until a human
checks"*. That is not what the code does. The UI inherits the confusion — the
detail sheet shows "Last checked 5 Aug 2026" for rows nobody has checked.

**Fix.** Split the column: `last_crawled_at` and `last_verified_at`, and let the
UI say which it means. **Effort: half a day**, including the migration and
backfill.

### H6 · Two rows are the same programme twice; fourteen look like it — **high**

> **Corrected 31 October 2026.** As first written, this finding said "a third of
> the catalogue is the same programme listed twice" and called the four-row
> Chevening scholarship group `certain`. Both overstate it. All 14 pages have
> since been read and 10 of the 12 pairs are separate awards —
> `duplicate-verdicts.md` has the verdicts and the quotes. The measurement below
> stands; the conclusion drawn from it did not.

Using the app's own detector (`findDuplicates()` from `src/lib/duplicates.ts`,
the one behind `/admin/duplicates`): **5 groups covering 14 of 43 rows (33%)**.
Reading the funder pages, **2 rows are genuinely duplicated** — the catalogue
goes 43 → 41.

- **one programme twice** — `Chevening Scholarships` (the `/who-can-apply/`
  sub-page) and `Chevening Scholarship`; `Humphrey Fellowship Program` and
  `Hubert H. Humphrey Fellowship Program`
- **separate awards the detector grouped** — Chevening SIDS, Chevening in
  Australia, Chevening LSE, Chevening OCIS, Distinguished Humphrey, Language
  Study vs Foreign Language and Area Studies, Fulbright Foreign Student vs FLTA

Where the overstatement came from is worth recording, because it is a trap the
reviewer walks into as well. `DuplicateCluster.confidence` is documented as
"the strongest pair in the cluster, for ordering the review queue", and the
four-row Chevening group earns `certain` from **one** pair inside it. The card
renders that cluster-level word as its badge. Three of those four rows are
different awards, and one of them — Chevening OCIS, whose eligibility list names
Indonesia — is the only row in its group an Indonesian can apply for.

The `likely` rule (*same site, and one title is the other plus a qualifier*) was
right once in six here. That matters beyond the review queue: `seed-crawl.ts`
treats `likely` as "already known" and declines to stage, so discovery would now
silently refuse to re-add five real programmes, with nothing recording the drop.

The machinery to fix this shipped (migration 014, `merge_scholarships`,
`/admin/duplicates`). It has never been used, and `possible_duplicate_scholarships`
returns 0 rows — measured 31 October as both `anon` and `service_role`. **The
reason given here earlier was wrong.** The view does intend to strip plurals;
its own comment at `008_normalise_countries.sql:62-63` names this exact pair.
It fails because the pattern is `'s\b'`, and in PostgreSQL's regex flavour `\b`
is a *backspace character*, not a word boundary — that is `\y` ([Table 9.20 and
9.22](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-matching.html)). The
de-pluralisation never fires, so `chevening scholarship` and
`chevening scholarships` never meet. The view is unused by the app, so this costs
nothing today beyond a misleading `select`.

**Fix.** Two merges at `/admin/duplicates`, named in `duplicate-verdicts.md`.
**Effort: 10 minutes**, now that the judgement is done. Note that no key in
`.env.local` can perform them: `merge_scholarships` checks `is_admin()`, which
reads `auth.jwt() ->> 'email'`, so it needs a signed-in admin browser session.

## 4. Extraction pipeline

**How it runs.** One pipeline, two hosts: `npm run extract` with the
service-role key, and `/api/admin/pipeline` + `/tick` driven by an admin's own
session. Both call `extractProgramme()` (`scripts/extract/programme.ts:205`).
Scheduling is the single weekly Vercel cron. No GitHub Actions, no queue.

**Providers.** Preference order `gemini → groq → anthropic`
(`providers/index.ts:10`); Gemini default `gemini-flash-lite-latest`, 15 rpm;
Groq `llama-3.3-70b-versatile`, 12k tpm; Anthropic `claude-opus-5`, no limiter
because "the constraint is cost, not a rate limit". Escalation is opt-in and has
**never run** — `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is not configured. All 141 extractions are
Gemini (139) or Groq (2).

**How failures surface: mostly they do not.** 11 of 23 jobs died on
`Gemini daily quota reached` — the dominant failure mode by a distance. They are
recorded in `pipeline_jobs.error` and visible at `/admin/pipeline` if somebody
looks. Nothing alerts.

**Cost and token use: not tracked where it matters.** `run.ts:352-360`
accumulates tokens in memory for the CLI and prints an estimate with a
**hard-coded** Anthropic price at `run.ts:444-452`. `src/lib/pipeline/runner.ts`
never passes `record`, so browser-run and cron-run extractions — i.e. every
unattended run — account for nothing. There is no token table, no cost column,
no cap. **Medium.**

**How quotes are matched.** `findQuote()` (`quote.ts:203-239`): exact substring
across all pages first, then a sliding-window loose match requiring ≥85% of the
quote's tokens *and every number in the quote* present. `MIN_QUOTE_CHARS = 8`,
`MIN_LOOSE_TOKENS = 6`. Normalisation is one function (`page.ts:103-112`):
NBSP, curly quotes, the whole dash range, whitespace collapse, lowercase.

Layered on top, and this is the good part: ISO-shape checks, **date precision**
(a quote saying "September 2026" cannot license a day), **cycle age** (a date
more than 365 days before the fetch is refused — this is what correctly rejected
Richard Rockefeller's 2019 deadline), **number support** (every figure in a
value must appear in its own quote), and per-item list matching. 327 unit tests
cover these, and `docs/testing.md` is unusually honest about what they do not.

**M12 · Non-English pages get exact matching only — medium.** `TOKEN =
/[a-z]+|\d+(?:[.,]\d+)*/g` (`quote.ts:94`) matches ASCII only, so CJK, Thai,
Arabic and Cyrillic text yields zero tokens and the loose matcher is
structurally unavailable; `datePrecision` knows English month names only. There
is also **no Unicode NFC/NFKC normalisation anywhere**, so a decomposed `é`
fails an exact match against a precomposed one. For a product whose catalogue
includes Japan, Korea and China this is a real ceiling.

**PDFs and JavaScript pages** are handled well — `unpdf`, a 12 MB cap, 40 pages,
OCR via an optional service with its own `match: "ocr"` evidence tier; and a
four-rung hydration ladder (`__NEXT_DATA__`/flight payloads → `<noscript>` → AMP
→ an optional render service). Neither OCR nor the render service is configured,
so a JS shell like NUS's page simply fails — as it did in the sample.

**Provider comparison on `eval/provider-comparison`:** the script exists
(`scripts/eval/compare-providers.ts`, 325 lines, read-only, with a `--max-cost`
guard), `scripts/eval/results/.gitignore` excludes its output, and no results are
committed. **It has never been run.** Running it needs an Anthropic key and real
spend, which you asked me not to do.

## 5. Security

### C2 · Next.js 16.2.12 carries two critical unauthenticated RCE advisories — **critical**

`npm audit`: **21 advisories — 2 critical, 13 high, 5 moderate, 1 low.** The two
critical ones are both in `next` ≤16.4.0: unauthenticated RCE on Windows-hosted
servers (not applicable on Vercel) and **unauthenticated RCE in the Image
Optimization API when AVIF files are used**. `/_next/image` answers 400 rather
than 404 on the deployment, so the route is live even though the app uses no
`next/image`. The fix is `next@16.4.3` — a patch-level bump. **Effort: 30
minutes plus a smoke test.**

### H2 · Unreviewed model output is readable by anyone — **high**

RLS is enabled on every table and the policies are right. But two views were
created without `security_invoker`, and a view reads its underlying tables with
its *owner's* rights — the exact trap `docs/architecture.md:136-142` documents
and warns against. Measured against the live project with the published anon key
and no session:

| Relation | rows to an anonymous caller | of total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scholarships` | 43 | 43 — intended, this is the public read path |
| `scholarship_completeness` | 43 | 43 — `security_invoker` set, inherits the public read |
| **`extraction_review`** | **5** | 5 — **leak** |
| **`scholarships_needing_review`** | **18** | 18 — **leak** |
| `scholarship_extractions` | 0 | 141 — policy holding |
| `admins`, `user_targets`, `pipeline_jobs`, `pipeline_job_lines`, `scholarship_candidates` | 0 | — policy holding |

`extraction_review` hands out unreviewed proposals with their quotes, model
notes and confidence — for example a `proposed_deadline` of `2027-02-12` with
the quote *"apply by February 12, 2027, at 11:59 p.m."*, a value deliberately
**not** applied to the catalogue. The product's whole discipline is that nothing
reaches a student until a human applies it; this publishes it through the same
API your Hermes agent reads. `possible_duplicate_scholarships` and
`scholarship_provenance` have the same omission and are currently empty.

**Fix.** `ALTER VIEW … SET (security_invoker = true)` on all four. **Effort: 30
minutes.** This is a production schema change, so it needs your go-ahead.

### Other security findings

- **M10 · No rate limiting anywhere.** No limiter on any route. The sharpest
  edge is `signInAnonymously()` in `src/lib/supabase/session.ts:48`, called from
  the proxy on every unauthenticated request — each one mints a real Supabase
  user. Both email-sending Server Actions are bounded only by Supabase's own
  limits. **Medium.**
- **M6 · Prompt injection: fences are not escaped.** Page text goes into the
  model between `--- BEGIN EXCERPTS ---` / `--- END EXCERPTS ---` and
  `--- PAGE: <url> ---` (`extract.ts:298`, `passages.ts:190`), and nothing strips
  or escapes those strings from the page's own text, so a hostile page can close
  the fence or forge a page attribution. No instruction tells the model the
  block is untrusted data. **The blast radius is genuinely small** — tag
  stripping removes hidden HTML payloads, JSON-LD goes through a 24-key
  allowlist, passage selection is keyword-gated, output is re-validated with Zod,
  and anything that survives must still pass grounding and then a human — but an
  attacker who controls a funder's page controls the quote too. **Medium.**
- **L7 · SSRF, admin-reachable only.** The fetch layer checks robots.txt and the
  `https?:` scheme and nothing else — no private-IP, loopback or metadata guard
  (`page.ts:365-374`). A row containing `http://169.254.169.254/…` would be
  fetched from the Vercel function. Writing that row needs admin RLS, so this is
  not anonymously reachable. **Low**, but cheap to close.
- **M5 · `shadcn` is a production dependency.** `package.json:30` lists the
  shadcn CLI as a runtime dependency because `globals.css:3` imports
  `shadcn/tailwind.css`. It drags `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk` (6 high
  advisories), `hono`, `@hono/node-server` and `systeminformation` into the
  production tree. The app uses none of them. **Medium** — vendor the stylesheet
  and move shadcn to devDependencies.
- **Good, and worth saying.** The service-role key appears nowhere under `src/`
  (one comment aside) and is never `NEXT_PUBLIC_`. The cron route signs in as an
  ordinary admin account rather than using the service key — deliberate and well
  argued at `cron/refresh/route.ts:19-32`. `is_admin()` resolves the email from
  `auth.users` rather than the JWT, so a stale claim cannot grant admin. All four
  `/api/admin/*` routes check server-side and return 403; `/api/cron/refresh`
  fails closed without its secret. No route accepts a user-supplied URL.

### H5 · Nothing runs any of that automatically — **high**

**There is no `.github/` directory.** No pre-commit hook, no branch protection
that could depend on a check. `npm run check` exists, passes, and is purely a
local courtesy. For a project whose correctness story rests on 327 regression
tests drawn from real failures, that is the gap that lets the next one through.

To be precise about what *does* run: opening a pull request fires two Vercel
checks — "Vercel Preview Comments" and a preview deployment. Those build the
app, so a PR that does not compile is caught. They run **no lint, no typecheck
and no tests**. (Confirmed on the PR carrying this audit.)

The silver lining is that **a preview environment already exists** — Phase 4
asks for one and Vercel is giving you a per-PR deployment for free. What is
missing is the gate in front of it.

**Effort: 1 hour** for a workflow running `npm run check` on push and PR.

**L1 · The crawler is never linted.** `"lint": "eslint src"` — `scripts/` is ~5k
lines including every grounding control, and ESLint never sees it. `tsc` does
cover it (`tsconfig.json:32-39`).

### Documentation drift — **medium**

## 7. Product and UX

Screenshots: [`audit-2026-10-30/screens/`](audit-2026-10-30/screens/).

**The craft is high.** The empty state reads *"Your filters are stricter than the

### H7 · The first screen is a wall of closed programmes — **high**

The default sort is "Deadline (soonest first)" and closed programmes are **not**
hidden by default, so every card above the fold reads "Cycle has rolled over" —

exists and is off by default.

**Fix.** Default `upcomingOnly` on, or sort open-before-closed. **Effort: under
an hour**, and it changes the product's first impression more than anything else
on this list.

### Other UX findings

- **M8 · The first visit can fail.** A cold request to `/` 307s to `/dashboard`
  while minting an anonymous session, and the first render produced "Something

  Reproduced once, on a fresh browser profile (screenshot:
  `first-visit-error-1440.jpg`). For a first-time visitor that is the whole
  product. Worth reproducing deliberately before fixing.
- **M4 · No scholarship has a URL.** Details open in a client-side sheet

  bookmarked or indexed. For a tool students send each other, that is a product
  gap as much as an SEO one.

- **Filters are strong.** Country, degree, four eligibility toggles with honest

  IELTS/TOEFL *threshold* filter, no field-of-study filter (only 16% of rows
  have the data), and the country list is just the seven countries the catalogue

### Performance — one problem, and it is server-side

Four runs against the live site (not Lighthouse — `npx lighthouse` was
unavailable in the audit sandbox, so these come from the Performance API and are
single-machine, warm-cache, directional):

| | |
| --- | --- |
| TTFB | 19–76 ms |
| **HTML stream complete** | **2,986 – 3,531 ms** |
| LCP | **3,128 ms** |
| CLS | 0 |

The edge answers in under 80 ms and then the page takes three seconds to

**Medium.** The fix is caching the catalogue read — this is exactly what Next 16's
caching APIs are for, and `node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/01-getting-started/08-caching.md`

## 9. Legal and ethics

**Crawl posture is clean and genuinely careful.** All 15 hosts checked
(`scripts/audit/robots-posture.ts`): **0 disallow the pages we cite.** Three
declare a `Crawl-delay` — Stanford 30s, pdsoros.org 10s, rotary.org 10s — and the
crawler honours all three, dropping to a single connection for any host that
declares one (`page.ts:637-645`). The floor is 800 ms per host, two connections.
The robots parser is a real one with `Allow`, longest-match-wins and `$`
anchoring, not a `Disallow` grep, and it is enforced in the fetch layer so no
caller can skip it.

Two things to tidy:

- **L2 · The user agent's contact URL is a placeholder** — `ScholarTrackBot/1.0
  (scholarship deadline verification; +https://github.com/)`. A webmaster who
  wants to reach you cannot. Point it at a real page. **Effort: 5 minutes.**
- Rotary returns **429** despite the honoured delay, so something other than rate
  is blocking us. Worth an email rather than a workaround.

**Copyright.** Quotes are short, factual, attributed to their source URL, and
used to support a fact rather than to republish the page — comfortably within
fair dealing. The one thing to watch is `document_checklist`, which sometimes
reproduces a funder's whole list of required documents. Keep an eye on length
rather than on count.

**M9 · Privacy — the gap is the paperwork, not the practice.** No privacy
policy, no terms, no cookie notice. The app sets
`sb-<project>-auth-token` with `Max-Age=34560000` (400 days) on the **first
request**, before any interaction, because the proxy mints an anonymous Supabase
user for every visitor. There is no analytics and no third-party tracker, which
is the hard part done — but a 400-day identifier placed before consent, with no
policy explaining it, is the kind of thing that is trivially fixable now and
awkward later. Given that the tracker is the only reason the session exists, the
cleaner answer may be not to mint one until the user tracks something (see §11).

**The disclaimer** exists on every detail sheet and nowhere else. For a product
that tells people when to apply for things, it belongs in the footer and on the
dashboard too.

## 11. The questions you raised

**Experiment → Beta on rangkalabs.cloud.** Proposed, measurable, all checkable
from the repo:

1. **≥90% of rows re-verified within the last 30 days** (today: 0%).
2. **≥95% of stored quotes still found on their page**, measured by
   `scripts/audit/quote-drift.ts` (today: 89% overall, **57% for `deadline_date`**).
3. **Per-field accuracy ≥95% on a 50-record hand-labelled eval set**, with the
   set committed and the number published (today: no eval set exists).
4. **100% of rows state Indonesian eligibility explicitly** (today: 9 of 43 state
   any nationality restriction).
5. **≥18 of the 21 major programmes held** (today: 7).
6. **Zero rows whose deadline has passed without a recorded re-read.**
7. CI green, no critical advisories, an alert on pipeline silence.

Items 1, 2 and 7 are mostly C1. Item 3 is the real work.

**Do you need accounts?** On this evidence, no — not yet. `user_targets` holds
**2 rows**. The anonymous session is minted for every visitor to support a
feature almost nobody uses, and it is the only reason you need a privacy policy,
a cookie story and an auth surface at all. Local-only saving (localStorage) with
an optional export would drop the privacy burden to near zero, remove the
unbounded `signInAnonymously()` surface, and let you delete a meaningful amount
of code. The argument against is that cross-device tracking is a real feature for
a year-long application cycle — but two rows says nobody is asking for it yet. I
would make the session lazy first (mint on first track, not on first visit), keep
the data, and revisit when there is demand.

**Notes for "ScholarTrack: no quote, no value".** Everything is in
`docs/audit-2026-10-30/` and regenerable from `scripts/audit/`. The numbers the
piece wants:

- 1,732 grounded quotes re-tested; 89% still on the page; **0 fabrications found**.
- Drift by field family: eligibility 1%, documents 3%, coverage 6%, timeline
  dates 15%, **row dates 41%**, `deadline_date` **43%**.
- The named mechanism: 73 of 109 date quotes gone from one Chevening page, 18 of
  18 from one Harvard page, because the pages rolled to the next cycle.
- The honest counter-example: Richard Rockefeller, where grounding worked
  perfectly, the `CYCLE_DAYS = 365` rule correctly refused a 2019 deadline, and
  the row still should not be in the catalogue. Grounding is not curation.
- The thing I got wrong, which is a better story than the thing I got right: I
  was certain "Chevening LSE Fellowship, China only" was a mis-attributed quote
  from a listing page. The fellowship really is China-only. The quote check was
  right and the auditor was wrong.

**One more thing a serious scholarship platform needs**, not on your list: **a
correction path with an SLA**. You plan a "report wrong data" button for Phase 3.
The part that matters is not the button — it is committing publicly to how fast a
report gets looked at, and showing the date each record was last checked next to
it. That is what turns "we verify" from a claim into a practice, and it is the
same discipline as "no quote, no value" applied to the humans.
